Transport maths · fixed prices

Walk / cycle to On; buy transit for actual use.

A personal 1–2-zone ZVV monthly pass is CHF 88. A full-price 1–2-zone single is CHF 4.60; six journeys cost CHF 26.80. The pass starts to make sense at roughly ten return city journeys in a month—so do not buy it by reflex if work is mostly walkable.

Outside, properly

Hills, forest and views without a big-spend weekend.

Cost rule: every route below is free once you arrive. Walk or cycle the local ones; for the others, check the ZVV fare in the live planner before leaving rather than automatically buying a day pass. Pack water, snacks and a layer—view restaurants are optional, not part of the trip.

Big city, lake and Alpine views from Käferberg above Höngg. Go for sunset, but leave enough light for the return. The outdoor lookout is public all day; take a live bus connection when you want to save your legs, or walk up through Wipkingen.

The proper mountain · free trail

Uetliberg

Zürich’s home mountain is the fog-escape move in November: forest, city-and-lake views and enough paths to make it a real outing. The summit and trails are free. Current works mean the S10 only runs as far as Selnau until December 2026—use the live planner for the replacement route.

THE “NOT TECHNO” RULE

Read the small print.

Green flags: house, disco, nu-disco, funk, soulful/jazzy house, R&B, Afro/organic house, live soul. Yellow flags: “electronic” without a lineup. Skip nights tagged hard techno, industrial or big-room if that is not your thing. A named DJ/collective beats a vague flyer every time.

Best pre-plan: check Thursday/Friday listings, then buy a ticket only for a sound you actually want.

Outside Zürich · weekends that feel far away

Cabin energy without an epic journey.

These are strong outdoorsy bases for a proper overnight or two. Travel times are planning estimates from Zürich HB; add the short trip from Josefstrasse and check the live connection, mountain railway and trail condition before booking.

Best cabin-feel weekend · ~1h45 + funicular

Braunwald, Glarus

Car-free village, actual chalets, steep valley walls and the kind of quiet that makes a Friday night train worth it. The Braunwaldbahn takes seven minutes from Linthal, which Zürich reaches in about 1½ hours. Best all-round choice for a real two-night reset.

Access: village year-round · check the Braunwaldbahn, hotel and trail status before leaving.

Snow + mountain base · under 1h30

Flumserberg

The straightforward winter answer: alpine lodging, snow and the Churfirsten ridge above Walensee without a massive journey. It is more resort than hidden village, but excellent when you want snow, winter walking or skiing with minimum logistics.

Access: lifts and winter activities are seasonal; verify operating status and last return before booking.

Big-view mountain village · ~1h45

Stoos

The steep funicular delivers you into a car-free alpine village at around 1,300m: winter walking, snowshoeing and proper mountain panorama without having to drive. More dramatic and more organised than Braunwald; book early for a Saturday stay.

Funicular: runs year-round except revisions · lifts and ridge routes are seasonal.

Quiet winter walk · ~1h30–1h45

Amden / Arvenbüel

Less showy, more wood-and-snow-country: above Walensee, with rural guesthouse energy and excellent open views across the Churfirsten and Toggenburg. Choose this for hiking, a quiet stay and a packed lunch—not a nightlife weekend.

Outdoor routes: weather and snow dependent; check the local trail report before committing.

Full alpine weekend · ~1h45–2h

Engelberg

The most developed option on this list: serious mountains, many accommodation choices and a proper resort atmosphere. Use it when the weather is good enough to justify the bigger spend, or when you want skiing and winter terrain rather than just a quiet cabin.

Access: train and mountain operations are seasonal; pre-check Titlis/Brunni conditions and accommodation.

Close, low-commitment reset · ~1h

Zugerberg

Not a full cabin escape unless you deliberately stay in Zug, but the quickest way to trade the city for forest, views and proper air. The funicular takes eight minutes from Schönegg; it is ideal for a long Sunday or a no-friction Saturday outdoors.

Access: funicular and outdoor trails year-round; winter sport depends on snow.

Alpstein views + a characterful town · ~1h30

Appenzell

For rolling hills, painted houses, local inns and an easy base for the Alpstein. It is a softer, more social mountain weekend than Stoos or Braunwald, and works brilliantly when you want excellent walks without committing to a big resort.

Access: village and train year-round; mountain routes are weather- and season-dependent.

Wood-and-snow village · ~1h30–1h45

Wildhaus, Toggenburg

A much quieter chalet option beneath the Churfirsten: winter walking, cross-country trails and slopes when snow arrives, then straightforward hiking in warmer months. Stay here for low-key mountain time rather than a polished resort scene.

Access: buses run year-round; lifts, snow routes and hiking conditions are seasonal.

Lake + mountain overnight · ~1h30–2h

Rigi / Vitznau

Take a lakeside inn in Vitznau or Weggis, then use the rack railway for a big-view mountain day. It is less cabin-rustic than Braunwald but ideal when you want water, a good dinner and a mountain morning in the same short weekend.

Access: railways operate to their published timetable; boat services and hiking options vary by season.

High valley + proper reset · ~2h30

Scuol, Engadin

A real change of scene: Engadin light, Romanesque village character and a thermal-bath recovery option after a long walk or ski day. The train journey is part of the appeal, so make this a two-night trip rather than racing back on Saturday.

Access: rail service year-round; spa, lifts and trails run to their own seasonal schedules.

Longer classic Alps stay · ~2h30–2h45

Kandersteg

The old-school Alpine-valley choice: traditional hotels, big walking country and a less glossy feel than the famous Jungfrau villages. It earns its longer train time when you want a proper mountain weekend, not just a quick view.

Access: rail year-round; Oeschinensee, lifts and mountain paths depend on season and conditions.

Best order: start with Braunwald for the cabin weekend, Appenzell or Wildhaus for an easy one-night reset, Flumserberg for snow, Stoos for a big-view splurge, then Scuol or Kandersteg when you want the trip to feel genuinely away. For any Friday–Sunday stay, compare direct booking with the local tourism site and reserve transport/accommodation early rather than paying last-minute Zürich prices.

The money rule

Price the whole weekend, not the flight.

London, Amsterdam and Copenhagen can make a cheap fare irrelevant once accommodation is added. Mallorca, Nice and Lisbon can be better-value shoulder-season weekends even when the ticket is higher.

The time rule

Only book flights that protect the weekend.

Check the live route from Josefstrasse and leave enough margin for baggage and security. A slightly pricier flight that lands early Friday or leaves late Sunday can be the much better deal.

The alternative

Compare rail before a short-hop flight.

For nearby European cities, the airport time can erase the air-time advantage. Use the SBB international planner before booking, especially when you care more about the journey than a city-break checklist.

NOVEMBER–DECEMBER

Warm, rainproof basics.

A proper coat, knitwear, waterproof shoe or boot, scarf and compact umbrella matter more than a big wardrobe. The key is staying dry on the walk to work or the tram.

JANUARY–FEBRUARY

Make cold days boringly easy.

Keep gloves, a warm layer and a reliable waterproof outer shell by the door. You do not need alpine gear for city life—just footwear that works when pavements are wet or cold.

MARCH

Layers return, rain stays.

Swap the heaviest layer for a lighter coat over knitwear, but do not pack away rain protection. This is when walking and cycling become much more attractive again.

APRIL

Buy nothing until it is truly missing.

Keep the useful rain layer and one warm mid-layer. Spend on a replacement only after a real gap appears; second-hand clothing is for a specific need, not a new-city shopping spree.

Important, not a tax calculator

There is no safe “183 days in Zürich” shortcut.

In Zürich, a stay of at least 30 days with gainful employment, or 90 days without it, can establish Swiss tax residence. But the tax domicile normally follows the centre of life and is case-specific—not a number you can choose.

The Netherlands also looks at the overall facts: where you spend time, work, live, are insured and keep your social/economic ties. Keeping the Amsterdam flat makes that worth checking properly.

A six-month rhythm

Make the good life the repeatable one.

MON–THU

Work, home food, short local plans.

Walk or cycle when it is easy, use Aldi/Lidl for the proper shop, and keep the everyday evenings close to Kreis 5.

ONE WORKDAY

Spend on a lunch you chose.

Take the occasional proper lunch with a colleague; do not turn every rushed office day into a CHF 25 default.

ONE EVENING

Make it worth leaving home.

Free art, a film at RiffRaff or one good bar is enough. Check the return route before you leave and cap the night before taxis take over.

WEEKEND

Use the hills, water or a planned trip.

Waid, Käferberg, Uetliberg and the Limmat are the default. Pay for one thing that adds to the week, not a string of interchangeable spends.
